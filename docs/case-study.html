Case Study

2022 FIFA World Cup — preliminary statistical analysis plan

Question and audience

Research question: How well can we predict the number of goals scored by a soccer team in a match from shots, completed passes, corner kicks, fouls committed, and completed dribbles?

Audience: Soccer fans, coaches, and managers who want to understand which recorded match statistics are most strongly associated with goal scoring.

Dataset and unit of analysis

The study uses StatsBomb Open Data for the 2022 FIFA World Cup. StatsBomb records event-level match information, including event type, time, team, player, and event-specific outcomes. We plan to aggregate those events to one observation per team per match.

Source: StatsBomb open-data information.

Response and predictors

Role Variable Team-match definition
Response Goals Number of shots recorded as goals
Predictor Shots Total shot events
Predictor Completed passes Pass events without an unsuccessful outcome
Predictor Corners Pass events classified as corner kicks
Predictor Fouls Foul-committed events
Predictor Completed dribbles Dribble events classified as completed

Proposed baseline model

We begin with multiple linear regression:

Goals = β0 + β1(Shots) + β2(CompletedPasses) + β3(Corners) + β4(Fouls) + β5(CompletedDribbles) + ε.

The original proposal includes R code to download match-event data, calculate team-match variables, plot the marginal associations, and fit the baseline model. The source is retained in source/original_proposal.qmd in the repository.

Proposed analyses

  1. Examine scatterplots and the relationship of each predictor with goals.
  2. Fit the baseline multiple linear regression model.
  3. Examine residuals, influential observations, and multicollinearity.
  4. Compare the baseline with LASSO and ridge regression using cross-validation; consider a count-response model such as Poisson regression if assumptions justify it.

Important limitations

This is an observational dataset: coefficients describe associations, not causal effects. Goals are nonnegative counts, so ordinary least squares assumptions may be imperfect. Related attacking statistics could also be correlated with each other. Results from the 2022 World Cup may not generalize to other levels of soccer.

Work in progress

The project is not final. We have not published final figures, diagnostic results, predictive performance comparisons, or conclusions on this page.
